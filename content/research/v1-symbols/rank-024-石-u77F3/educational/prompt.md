# Educational origin illustration brief: 石

Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: educational origin reconstruction for the Script Roots Symbol Journey

## Primary request

Illustrate the concrete object, action, body form, or meaningful scene behind the earliest defensible story for 石. The composition must be understandable as a real-world referent first, then visually echo the silhouette, arrangement, or meaningful parts documented in the historical evidence. The learner should be able to compare this illustration with the separately displayed earliest glyph and understand the proposed transition without the illustration pretending to be the glyph.

## Source cues

A rock 口 at the base of a cliff 厂 / Pictograph of a stone chime. / 山石也。在厂之下；囗，象形。凡石之屬皆从石。 / Pictogram (象形): a stone beneath a cliff (厂). The cliff was subsequently distorted into 丆; compare 叚, 段 as well as 度, 席.
Alternatively, a cave set into the side of a cliff or mountain.
Unknown.
Schuessler, (2007) proposes a probable relation Proto-Vietic *l-taːʔ (“stone”) (whence Vietnamese đá and Khmer ដា (daa, “rock; stone”)); however this is unlikely due to both:
* the mismatch between final *-k and *-ʔ, which requires Schuessler to assume that Chinese added "the familiar final -k" after foreign *-ʔ, resulting in *-ʔ-k > *-k (for an analogy, 汝 (OC *naʔ) > 若 (OC *nak)), therefore reducing his proposal's parsimony;
* that so far only nasal pre-initials, not *-l-, are known to have a voicing effect on Old Chinese voiceless initials (see Baxter & Sagart, 2014, Jacques, 2020, etc.); so Proto-Vietic *l-t- would unlikely become Old Chinese *d-. / Borrowed from 擔／担 (dàn), after the shoulder pole used to carry such a mass of grain (Qiu, 1988, p. 220).

## Composition constraints

- Keep the meaningful object or relationship visually dominant and uncluttered.
- If the formation is multi-component, show each meaning-bearing component in a single coherent scene.
- Do not invent a component meaning when the source marks it as phonetic.
- Do not draw Oracle Bone, Bronze, Seal, Clerical, or Regular glyphs inside the illustration. Those remain separate historical evidence assets.
- Use the approved warm paper, ink, muted mineral, and restrained editorial museum direction used by the existing 11-symbol concept art.
- No modern text, labels, watermark, decorative border, or square icon treatment.
