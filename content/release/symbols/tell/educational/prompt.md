# Educational origin illustration brief: 告

Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: educational origin reconstruction for the Script Roots Symbol Journey

## Primary request

Illustrate the concrete object, action, body form, or meaningful scene behind the earliest defensible story for 告. The composition must be understandable as a real-world referent first, then visually echo the silhouette, arrangement, or meaningful parts documented in the historical evidence. The learner should be able to compare this illustration with the separately displayed earliest glyph and understand the proposed transition without the illustration pretending to be the glyph.

## Source cues

To speak 口 with the force of an ox 牛 / Pictograph of a tongue extending from an open mouth. Graphically similar to 舌 (tongue). / 牛觸人，角箸橫木，所以告人也。从口从牛。《易》曰：“僮牛之告。”凡告之屬皆从告。 / Cognate with Thai กล่าว (glàao, “to say; to declare”) (Schuessler, 2007). / From 校 (MC kaewH, “to check; to compare”) (Yang, 2010).

## Composition constraints

- Keep the meaningful object or relationship visually dominant and uncluttered.
- If the formation is multi-component, show each meaning-bearing component in a single coherent scene.
- Do not invent a component meaning when the source marks it as phonetic.
- Do not draw Oracle Bone, Bronze, Seal, Clerical, or Regular glyphs inside the illustration. Those remain separate historical evidence assets.
- Use the approved warm paper, ink, muted mineral, and restrained editorial museum direction used by the existing 11-symbol concept art.
- No modern text, labels, watermark, decorative border, or square icon treatment.
