## Current visual-story evidence

A rock 口 at the base of a cliff 厂 / Pictograph of a stone chime. / 山石也。在厂之下；囗，象形。凡石之屬皆从石。 / Pictogram (象形): a stone beneath a cliff (厂). The cliff was subsequently distorted into 丆; compare 叚, 段 as well as 度, 席.
Alternatively, a cave set into the side of a cliff or mountain.
Unknown.
Schuessler, (2007) proposes a probable relation Proto-Vietic *l-taːʔ (“stone”) (whence Vietnamese đá and Khmer ដា (daa, “rock; stone”)); however this is unlikely due to both:
* the mismatch between final *-k and *-ʔ, which requires Schuessler to assume that Chinese added "the familiar final -k" after foreign *-ʔ, resulting in *-ʔ-k > *-k (for an analogy, 汝 (OC *naʔ) > 若 (OC *nak)), therefore reducing his proposal's parsimony;
* that so far only nasal pre-initials, not *-l-, are known to have a voicing effect on Old Chinese voiceless initials (see Baxter & Sagart, 2014, Jacques, 2020, etc.); so Proto-Vietic *l-t- would unlikely become Old Chinese *d-. / Borrowed from 擔／担 (dàn), after the shoulder pole used to carry such a mass of grain (Qiu, 1988, p. 220).
