# Educational origin illustration brief: 望

Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: educational origin reconstruction for the Script Roots Symbol Journey

## Primary request

Illustrate the concrete object, action, body form, or meaningful scene behind the earliest defensible story for 望. The composition must be understandable as a real-world referent first, then visually echo the silhouette, arrangement, or meaningful parts documented in the historical evidence. The learner should be able to compare this illustration with the separately displayed earliest glyph and understand the proposed transition without the illustration pretending to be the glyph.

## Source cues

A king 王 gazing at the moon 月; 亡 provides the pronunciation / Pictograph of a person standing up (𡈼) and gazing at the moon (月). 亡 is a sound component that was added later. / 出亡在外，望其還也。从亾，朢省聲。 / Originally written 𦣠, an ideogrammic compound (會意 /会意): 臣 (“eye”) + 𡈼 (“person standing on the ground”) – a person standing up and looking off into the distance. Later 月 (“moon”) was added to produce 朢.
The 臣 component is now written 亡, making the current form a phono-semantic compound (形聲 /形声, OC *maŋ, *maŋs): phonetic 亡 (OC *maŋ) + semantic 月 + semantic 𡈼.
From Proto-Sino-Tibetan *mV-taŋ (“to see”). Cognate with Burmese မြင် (mrang).

- Do not draw Oracle Bone, Bronze, Seal, Clerical, or Regular glyphs inside the illustration. Those remain separate historical evidence assets.
- Use the approved warm paper, ink, muted mineral, and restrained editorial museum direction used by the existing 11-symbol concept art.
