## Current visual-story evidence

A tiger 虍 standing on a rock 几; 虍 also provides the pronunciation / Pictograph of a tiger. / 山獸之君。从虍，虎足象人足。象形。凡虎之屬皆从虎。 / Pictogram (象形). 虍 represents the tiger's head. The torso has disappeared and the legs and the tail have transformed into 人 in the small seal script and later 儿 in the clerical script.
From Proto-Sino-Tibetan *k-la (“tiger”), from Proto-Austroasiatic *kla(ː)ʔ (“tiger”). Cognate with 菟 (OC *daː) in 於菟 (OC *qa daː, “tiger”). Compare Vietnamese khái ("tiger").
Hill (2019) compares it to Tibetan སྟག (stag, “tiger”); however, Zheng Zining deems that comparison "probably spurious".

This wording is source aggregation, not final editorial copy. Preserve disagreement when specialist sources differ, and do not describe a component as meaningful if the source identifies it as phonetic only.

## Editorial research review — 2026-09-14

The selected Oracle specimen and CUHK's ancient-character entry support a complete side-view tiger: head, body, legs, tail, and natural stripe/detail marks. A secondary local aggregation describes a tiger standing on a rock and proposes a later component analysis; that explanation is not supported by the selected Oracle specimen or by the independent CUHK description of the early form. The production target therefore remains one indivisible tiger and does not add a rock or split the animal into components.
