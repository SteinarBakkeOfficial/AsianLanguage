## Current visual-story evidence

Selected production evidence: the Oracle-bone pictograph shows a woman in side view, kneeling or sitting back on her heels, with both arms/hands crossed or folded in front of the chest. Its original meaning is woman / female. The modern character is a later abstraction of this pictographic figure; do not use a standing, walking, dancing, or arms-raised pose for the origin illustration.
Graphically cognate to 母 (mǔ, “mother”) and 毋, which has developed similarly, but also includes dots for nipples and has retained both breasts. Compare Egyptian 𓁐.
Similar but unrelated to 卩, in which the person kneeling has unspecified gender.
From Proto-Sino-Tibetan *naq (“woman”). Compare Tibetan ཉག་མོ (nyag mo, “woman”) (Hill, 2019).
Pronunciation 2 is an exoactive derivation of pronunciation 1 (Schuessler, 2007).
