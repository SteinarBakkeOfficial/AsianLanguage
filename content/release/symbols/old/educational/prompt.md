# Educational origin illustration brief: 老

Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: educational origin reconstruction for the Script Roots Symbol Journey

## Primary request

Illustrate the concrete object, action, body form, or meaningful scene behind the earliest defensible story for 老. The composition must be understandable as a real-world referent first, then visually echo the silhouette, arrangement, or meaningful parts documented in the historical evidence. The learner should be able to compare this illustration with the separately displayed earliest glyph and understand the proposed transition without the illustration pretending to be the glyph.

## Source cues

A person bent over with long hair 匕 and a crutch; compare 耂 / Pictograph of an old person with long hair bent over and leaning on a cane. / 考也。七十曰老。从人、毛、匕。言須髮變白也。凡老之屬皆从老。 / Ideogrammic compound (會意 /会意): 人 (“man”) + 毛 (“hair”) + 匕 (“cane”) – a man with long hair (an old man), leaning on a cane. Compare top component to 孝 (OC *qʰruːs).
Cognate to 考 (OC *kʰluːʔ); the most commonly cited example of 轉注 (“reciprocal meaning”).
Unknown. Compare Proto-Sino-Tibetan *s-raw (“withered, residue, corpse”), *rwat (“stiff, tough”), whence Burmese ရော် (rau, “wither, become overripe; age”), ရွတ် (rwat, “old, stiff, tough”). See also 古 (gǔ), 故 (gù).
An old Sino-Vietnamese borrowing is rệu (“overripe, pulpy”).

## Composition constraints

- Keep the meaningful object or relationship visually dominant and uncluttered.
- If the formation is multi-component, show each meaning-bearing component in a single coherent scene.
- Do not invent a component meaning when the source marks it as phonetic.
- Do not draw Oracle Bone, Bronze, Seal, Clerical, or Regular glyphs inside the illustration. Those remain separate historical evidence assets.
- Use the approved warm paper, ink, muted mineral, and restrained editorial museum direction used by the existing 11-symbol concept art.
- No modern text, labels, watermark, decorative border, or square icon treatment.
