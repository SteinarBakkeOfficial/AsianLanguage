# Shared CJK Allowed Character Universe

Status: discussion reference / corpus boundary. This document defines the proposed allowed-character universe for V1, V2, and later corpus selection. It does not approve any character for runtime content.

Date: 2026-09-02

## Rule

All product corpus candidates must come from this shared China–Japan–Korea character universe. A candidate is eligible only when the underlying character identity is represented in:

- Simplified Chinese;
- Traditional Chinese usage communities;
- Japanese Kanji; and
- Korean Hanja.

If the identity is absent from either Japanese Kanji or Korean Hanja, it is disqualified regardless of historical interest or teaching value. A later local glyph form does not automatically create a new identity: for example, `國` / `国` are regional forms of one shared character identity and should be represented as local forms of the same `Shared Character`.

“Shared” means shared character identity, not identical glyph shape, pronunciation, or meaning in every modern language. The local form, reading, meaning, and usage evidence must be stored separately for each track. A shared character may have a different Japanese or Korean word, pronunciation, or semantic use and still qualify.

## Source and verification basis

The primary corpus boundary is the Trilateral Cooperation Secretariat’s *808 Commonly Used Chinese Characters in China, Japan and the ROK*. TCS describes this as a set compiled from contemporary frequently-used-character tables in China, Japan, and Korea, and identifies 808 traditional characters in the shared publication. [TCS overview](https://www.tcs-asia.org/en/cooperation/mechanism.php?topics=4) · [TCS publications](https://www.tcs-asia.org/en/data/publications.php?gubun=4) · [TCS 808 publication](https://www.tcs-asia.org/data/etcData/PUB_jp_1689038202.pdf)

The exact character sequence below is a normalized transcription of the published 808 list, cross-checked against two accessible transcriptions. The sequence is an allowed-identity reference, not a substitute for row-level verification. Before any entry becomes runtime content, the preparation record must still include the current Japanese Kanji inventory evidence, current Korean Hanja inventory evidence, Chinese-track forms, and usage examples.

Cross-check transcriptions: [Aige Services transcription](https://www.aige.co.uk/resource/details.asp?id=5) · [Kangxi Dictionary transcription](https://www.kangxizidian5.com/zhongrihan808/)

The transcription contains exactly 808 unique Han characters. A comparison against the 173-entry provisional research bank found 158 matches after obvious Traditional/Simplified variant normalization. These 15 provisional candidates are outside this allowed universe and must not be selected for V1, V2, or V3: `兔 坐 宮 帶 房 森 牙 矢 禾 腹 臉 舟 象 雷 鹿`. This comparison is a boundary check, not a replacement for the required per-track inventory audit.

## How this controls future versions

- V1, V2, and V3 candidates must be members of this universe.
- The universe is not itself divided into release quotas or collections.
- V1 selection can prioritize history, visual teaching, component structure, and usage only after the Kanji/Hanja gate passes.
- Local creations and characters absent from either required inventory remain outside the product corpus, even if they are excellent teaching examples in one language.
- Regional variants remain one Shared Character when the identity mapping is defensible; the preparation record must show the mapping rather than silently treating forms as identical.

## Required audit fields for every allowed candidate

- shared identity and normalized reference form;
- Simplified Chinese form and evidence;
- Traditional Chinese form and evidence;
- Japanese Kanji form, inventory source, readings, and usage;
- Korean Hanja form, inventory source, reading, and usage;
- variant/mapping notes where forms differ;
- earliest verified historical stage and source;
- formation type and component relationships;
- V1/V2/V3 status, collection membership, and review state.
