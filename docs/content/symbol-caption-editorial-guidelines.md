# Symbol Caption Editorial Guidelines

These rules govern the short explanatory text beneath the Origin-to-Regular Museum stages for every approved runtime symbol.

## Origin / illustration

Explain what the origin illustration is intended to show. Name the meaningful component or components and explain, briefly, why their combination conveys the intended meaning. If the graph may be a sound borrowing, say so here in compact form: the pictured components suggest one idea, while the graph was borrowed by sound to write another word.

Do not repeat the entire later history here. Origin establishes the proposed source idea and any essential uncertainty.

The learner-facing Origin caption should use the full `origin.explanation` field, not the shorter `originAnchor` field. Descriptions must remain in English and must not insert Chinese characters as prose labels.

## Oracle Bone Script

The Origin illustration explains why the intended meaning was chosen. Oracle Bone is the first historical drawing, so its caption explains only what the selected carved form visibly preserves, obscures, adds, or removes relative to that starting point. Do not repeat the complete Origin lesson, claim that the glyph “matches” the illustration, or invent an unseen historical reason. Because carved pictographs are often difficult for a modern learner to recognize, use a short visible-form bridge by default; leave the caption empty only when the selected image is genuinely self-explanatory after Origin.

## Bronze, Seal, and Clerical Script

Describe only what changed from the preceding form or writing practice. Focus on visible structural changes, simplification, standardization, compression, rotation, or a shift in how components were written. Do not repeat the Origin interpretation or restate the full semantic explanation unless the meaning itself changed at that stage.

## Regular Script

Give the conclusion of the journey: explain how the form settled into Regular Script and whether the earlier structure or meaning remains visible, changed, or was replaced. This is a concise summary of the evolution, not a generic font description and not a repetition of every intermediate stage.

Do not show a historical method caption on Regular Script. The Regular explanatory caption uses the same Museum body-text treatment as the other stage explanations. Chinese characters are allowed in this concluding Regular Script explanation when they are useful; the no-Chinese-characters rule applies to the Oracle, Bronze, Seal, and Clerical descriptions.

## Editorial test

Read the stages in order and ask:

1. Does Origin explain the proposed picture/components, why they combine to express the meaning, and any essential sound borrowing or uncertainty?
2. Does Oracle add only a necessary clarification about the historical form, without repeating the Origin explanation or referring to the illustration?
3. Does each later historical stage describe only its change from the previous stage?
4. Does Regular conclude how the form settled and what remains visible today?

If a sentence belongs to a different stage, move or rewrite it rather than repeating it. Keep simple, self-evident pictographs short; reserve fuller wording for genuine uncertainty, component interaction, or a real semantic/structural change.
